# Study plans

Be realistic first. The CISA covers five domains, two of them technical and heavy, and most candidates study for several weeks or months. ISACA does not publish a recommended study time, and the right amount depends on how much of the material you use at work. The two plans below are for the **final week** and the **final 48 hours**: closing gaps, practising the format and arriving rested.

Both plans assume you start with the [5-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md).

## Before the final week

If you have more time, give each domain its own block, in proportion to its weight and your diagnostic result: Domains 4 and 5 usually need the most time. For each domain, read the guide, do its items, keep a miss log, and read the official topic list in the [ISACA content outline](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa/cisa-exam-content-outline). Finish each block with a mixed timed set.

## 7-day plan

Expect about 2 to 3 hours a day.

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Reread the [exam overview](test-overview.md), especially the auditor's point of view. Retake the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). Pick your two weakest domains. |
| 2 | Domain 1 | The audit process shapes every answer. Redo its 4 items. Rebuild the sampling and evidence tables from memory. |
| 3 | Domain 4 | Redo its 4 items. Write out the backup, recovery site and plan test tables. |
| 4 | Domain 5 | Redo its 4 items. Write out who holds which key, the cloud responsibility table and the attack-to-control table. |
| 5 | First timed run | A timed mixed set, such as the [free test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/cisa?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=cisa). Read the [timing guide](timing.md) first. Review every miss. |
| 6 | Domains 2 and 3 | Redo both item sets. Focus on governance roles, vendor controls, testing types and changeover methods. Then redo items for your weakest domain from day 5. |
| 7 | Light review and rest | Read the traps lists for all five domains, nothing else. Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). Stop by early evening. |

### How to review a missed item

For each miss, answer three questions in your miss log:

1. **Whose action was the right answer?** The auditor's, or management's? Many misses pick something management should do.
2. **Why is the key better, not just correct?** Bigger risk, more reliable evidence, earlier in the process?
3. **What will I check next time?** One habit, such as "report, do not fix" or "prefer independent evidence".

## 2-day plan

For when the exam is the day after tomorrow.

### Day 1: close the biggest gaps

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 min | Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | 60 min | Read "Key concepts" and "Traps" for your two weakest domains. |
| 3 | 15 min | Do those two domains' items and read every explanation. |
| 4 | 60 min | A timed mixed set, such as the [free PrepClubs test](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/cisa?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=cisa). |
| 5 | 30 min | Review every miss with the three questions above. |

### Day 2: the day before the exam

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 min | Reread "The auditor's point of view" in the [exam overview](test-overview.md#the-auditors-point-of-view). |
| 2 | 45 min | Skim all five traps lists. |
| 3 | 15 min | Read the [timing guide](timing.md) and the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). |
| 4 | Rest | Nothing new after mid-afternoon. |

## What not to do

- **Do not hunt for "real" exam questions.** ISACA's exam content is confidential, recalled questions are unreliable, and sharing exam content breaks ISACA's rules.
- **Do not skip Domains 4 and 5 because they feel technical.** They are 52% of the exam.
- **Do not study only definitions.** ISACA writes experience-based questions that test judgement.
- **Do not cram the night before.** Four hours of careful reading needs a rested brain.
