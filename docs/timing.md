# Timing and pacing

The CISA gives you 4 hours for 150 multiple-choice questions. That is more time per question than many exams, but the scenarios are wordy and several options often look reasonable, so time still runs out for candidates who reread every stem.

## The budget

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 4 hours (240 minutes) | ISACA |
| Questions | 150 | ISACA |
| Average per question | 96 seconds | Arithmetic |
| Penalty for wrong answers | None | ISACA |
| Breaks | The candidate guide allows a short break of up to ten minutes with the proctor's permission. The clock keeps running. | ISACA |

ISACA's candidate guide also advises budgeting your time so you can complete the whole exam. Check the current guide for your delivery mode, test centre or remote, before the day.

## A pacing plan

This is our study guidance, not an ISACA rule. Aim for about **85 seconds per question** on a first pass. That leaves roughly 25 minutes for a break and for any questions you mark to revisit, if your exam interface allows it. Check the tutorial at the start of the exam for how marking and review work.

| Checkpoint | You should be at about |
|---|---|
| 40 minutes | Question 28 |
| 80 minutes | Question 56 |
| 120 minutes | Question 85 |
| 160 minutes | Question 113 |
| 200 minutes | Question 141 |
| 215 minutes | Question 150, with time to spare |

## How to handle a hard question

1. **Read the last sentence first** so you know what is asked: FIRST, BEST, MOST, GREATEST concern.
2. **Put yourself in the auditor's seat.** Evaluate, evidence, report. Not design, fix or approve.
3. **Eliminate the options management would do.** They are usually distractors.
4. **Choose between the last two** by asking which addresses the biggest risk, or gives the most reliable evidence.
5. **Do not sink more than about two minutes** into one question. Choose your best answer, note it if you can, and move on. A blank scores nothing.

## Where time usually goes

- **Rereading long stems.** Read once, carefully, picking out who is acting, at what stage, and what the risk is.
- **Overthinking technical detail.** Domains 4 and 5 are technical, but the questions want judgement, not configuration steps.
- **Debating two good options.** Decide by the auditor's role and by risk, then move.
- **Long breaks.** The clock keeps running.

## Practise against the clock

Do practice sets in one sitting, timed, in blocks of at least 50 questions, so four hours of concentration feels familiar. The [free CISA practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/cisa?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=cisa) is a timed set you can use for this.
