# For financial auditors and accountants moving into IT audit

If you come from financial audit, internal audit or accounting, you already think like an auditor, which is the hardest habit for technical candidates to learn. Your gap is different: the technical content, and how IT controls connect to the financial statements you already know.

## What transfers directly

| Skill you have | Where it appears on the CISA |
|---|---|
| Professional scepticism | Every question about evidence and management claims |
| Evidence and its reliability | Domain 1: independent, direct, documented evidence beats inquiry |
| Sampling, attribute and variable | Domain 1: compliance versus substantive testing |
| Materiality and risk-based planning | Domain 1: audit risk model, audit universe, risk-based plans |
| Reporting findings and follow-up | Domain 1: condition, criteria, cause, effect, recommendation |
| Independence | Every question where the auditor is invited to design or approve |
| Segregation of duties | Domain 2: incompatible duties and compensating controls |
| Reconciliations and control totals | Domains 3 and 4: data conversion, interfaces, batch controls |

## What is new

- **IT general controls.** Access management, change management and IT operations underpin every automated control and every system-generated report you rely on. If change management fails, you cannot trust that an automated control still works as designed.
- **The technical domains.** Domains 4 (operations and resilience) and 5 (protection of information assets) together carry **52%** of the exam. Backups and recovery, networks, encryption, cloud responsibility and incident response are all tested.
- **Systems development.** Domain 3 asks about project governance, testing types, data migration and go-live methods.
- **Judgement about technology risk.** Questions ask which technical weakness is the GREATEST concern. That means ranking risks you may not have met in a financial audit.

## How IT controls link to the numbers

| Financial assertion | IT controls that support it |
|---|---|
| Completeness | Interface reconciliations, batch totals, sequence checks, job monitoring |
| Accuracy | Input validation, processing controls, change management that protects calculation logic |
| Existence and occurrence | Access controls and segregation so that only authorised people can create transactions |
| Cut-off | Job scheduling and timestamp controls |
| Integrity of reports | Report logic under change control, restricted access to report parameters |

When a question mentions a system that feeds the general ledger, think about which of these controls would fail and what it would do to the figures.

## How to close the gap

1. **Start with Domain 1 as a quick win.** Much of it will be familiar, and it trains the CISA vocabulary.
2. **Spend most of your time on Domains 4 and 5.** Learn the tables in each guide until you can rebuild them: backups, recovery sites, plan tests, encryption keys, cloud models, attacks and controls.
3. **Learn concepts, not commands.** You need to know what a firewall, a DMZ or a hash does and why, not how to configure one.
4. **Keep the auditor's lens on technical items.** The answer is still about evidence, risk and reporting, even when the topic is encryption.
5. **Use your scepticism.** Distractors often offer comforting but weak evidence, such as a vendor's assurance or a success log.

## Experience, waivers and the application

ISACA's rules, in summary:

- **Experience:** a minimum of five years of professional information systems auditing, control, assurance or security work experience, as described in the CISA job practice areas.
- **Waivers:** experience waivers are available for a maximum of three years. ISACA sets out which education and experience qualify in its application materials; check the current list before you rely on it.
- **Time window:** the experience must be gained within the ten years before your application date, and you must apply within five years of passing the exam.
- **CISA Associate:** since July 2025, ISACA offers a CISA Associate designation for ISACA members who have passed the exam but do not yet have the required experience. It has no CPE requirement, needs an active ISACA membership, and expires after four years or when you become CISA certified.
- **Keeping the CISA:** at least 120 CPE hours over each three-year period, with a minimum of 20 hours each year, plus following ISACA's Code of Professional Ethics and IS auditing standards.

Each rule is linked in [Sources](sources.md). Check ISACA's own pages before you apply, as the details change from time to time.

## Practise the technical domains early

Do not leave Domains 4 and 5 to the end. A timed mixed set, such as the [free CISA practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/cisa?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=cisa), will show you quickly how wide the technical gap is.
