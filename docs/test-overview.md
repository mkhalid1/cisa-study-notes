# The CISA exam explained

The CISA (Certified Information Systems Auditor) is ISACA's credential for people who audit, control, monitor and assess information technology and business systems. The exam tests whether you can think and act as an IS auditor across five domains: the audit process itself, governance, systems acquisition and development, operations and resilience, and protection of information assets.

## Quick facts

| | What ISACA says |
|---|---|
| Publisher | ISACA |
| Questions | 150 multiple-choice questions |
| Time | 4 hours (240 minutes) |
| Domains | 5 job practice domains |
| Score scale | 200 to 800, scaled |
| Passing score | 450 or higher |
| Unscored items | The exam includes pretest items that do not count. ISACA does not publish how many. |
| Penalty for wrong answers | None. Answer every question. |
| Delivery | PSI testing centres worldwide, or remote proctoring |
| Languages | English, Chinese (Simplified), Spanish, Japanese, French, German, Korean |
| Registration window | Six months from registration to sit the exam |
| Experience to certify | Five years in IS audit, control, assurance or security, with waivers of up to three years |

Sources: [ISACA CISA exam content outline](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa/cisa-exam-content-outline), [ISACA CISA page](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa) and the [ISACA Certification Exam Candidate Guide (PDF)](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/-/media/fa494652c5f149289af38cef18328650.ashx). Checked on 24 November 2026.

The question counts are our arithmetic on 150 questions, not figures ISACA publishes, and pretest items mean the scored split will differ a little. The point they make is real: Domains 4 and 5 together carry just over half the exam.

Each domain splits into a Part A and a Part B. The domain guides list every official topic. ISACA's outline also lists 43 task statements describing what a CISA does on the job.

## What the questions look like

ISACA publishes an item development guide for the people who write CISA questions. In our own words, it says:

- Every item is multiple choice, with a stem and four options: one correct answer (the key) and three distractors.
- The key must reflect current practice. Sometimes it is the only correct option; sometimes it is the BEST of those given.
- Stems often describe a situation the auditor is in.
- Items are written for a minimally competent candidate with three to five years of experience.
- Writers avoid negative questions (NOT, LEAST), true or false items, "all of the above", fill-in-the-blank, vendor-specific products, region-specific regulation, and questions that only test the meaning of a term. ISACA calls the CISA an experience-based exam.

That guide was last updated in October 2018, under an older version of the job practice, so treat it as a description of style rather than of current content. Source: [ISACA CISA Item Development Guide (PDF)](https://www.isaca.org/certifications/additional-resources/-/media/487125b6c4e14a638c3f52ca3f8af344.ashx).

## The auditor's point of view

Almost every item asks what the IS auditor should do, or what should concern the IS auditor most. That point of view decides many answers:

- The auditor **evaluates, evidences and reports**. Management **designs, implements and fixes**.
- The auditor works **risk first**: understand the area and its risks before testing.
- The auditor needs **sufficient, reliable evidence**, and prefers independent, direct, documented evidence.
- The auditor protects **independence and objectivity**.
- When something is wrong, the auditor **reports it** and **follows up**; the auditor does not take over.

Candidates who come from operations, security or management roles often pick the answer that fixes the problem. On the CISA, that is usually wrong.

## Who it is for

IS auditors, internal auditors, external audit staff who handle IT, risk and compliance professionals, and security staff who assess controls. If you come from financial audit or accounting, the [page for financial auditors](for-financial-auditors.md) covers what transfers and what is new.

## After the exam

ISACA shows a preliminary pass or fail on screen at the end, and emails the official score within ten working days. To become certified you then apply, within five years of passing, with evidence of your experience. See the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md#afterwards) and the [financial auditors page](for-financial-auditors.md#experience-waivers-and-the-application) for the rules.
