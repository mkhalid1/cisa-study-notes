# Frequently asked questions

These are the questions people search for most about the CISA, mostly drawn from Google's "People also ask" and "Things to know" results for US searches in November 2026, plus a few we hear often. Answers stick to what ISACA states, and say so when an answer is our guidance.

## What is CISA certification?

The Certified Information Systems Auditor is ISACA's credential for professionals who audit, control and assess information systems. To earn it you pass a 150-question exam covering five domains, then apply with evidence of five years of relevant experience, with waivers of up to three years available. See the [exam overview](test-overview.md).

## How long is the CISA exam?

Four hours (240 minutes) for 150 multiple-choice questions, about 96 seconds per question on average. See the [timing guide](timing.md).

## What is the passing score?

ISACA reports scores on a scale from 200 to 800, and 450 or higher is a pass. It is a scaled score, not a percentage of questions answered correctly. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## How difficult is it to pass the CISA exam?

It is demanding because it is broad and because questions test an auditor's judgement rather than definitions. ISACA writes questions for a candidate with three to five years of experience. Candidates from technical roles often find the auditor's point of view hardest; candidates from financial audit often find Domains 4 and 5 hardest.

## What is the pass rate for the CISA exam?

ISACA does not publish one. Figures quoted online are not official.

## What are the eligibility requirements for the CISA exam?

You can sit the exam before you have the experience. To be certified, ISACA requires five years of professional information systems auditing, control, assurance or security experience, gained within the ten years before you apply, with waivers of up to three years. You must apply within five years of passing. Since 2025, ISACA members who pass without the experience can hold the CISA Associate designation. See the [financial auditors page](for-financial-auditors.md#experience-waivers-and-the-application).

## How long does it take to prepare for the CISA exam?

It depends on your background, and ISACA does not publish a figure. As our guidance, plan for several weeks to a few months, with the most time on your weakest domains and on Domains 4 and 5, which carry 52% of the exam. The [study plans](study-plans.md) cover the final week and the final two days.

## How do I register for the CISA exam?

Register through ISACA's website. From the date you register, you have a six-month eligibility period to schedule and sit the exam, at a PSI test centre or by remote proctoring. Fees depend on ISACA membership status; check the current amounts on [ISACA's CISA page](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa).

## How much does it cost to get a CISA certificate?

There are several parts: the exam registration, which differs for ISACA members and non-members, a certification application fee after passing, and annual maintenance. Check the current figures on [ISACA's site](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa), since they vary and change.

## How do I become an IT auditor?

Common routes are internal or external audit, accounting, or IT operations and security, followed by audit work on IT controls. The CISA job practice is a good map of what IT auditors do: it lists 43 task statements across the five domains. Passing the CISA and building experience in those tasks is a recognised path.

## Which is harder, CIA or CISA?

They test different things. The CIA, from the Institute of Internal Auditors, covers internal audit broadly; the CISA focuses on information systems audit, control and security. Which feels harder depends on your background. Compare the two using each body's official exam outline.

## Is CISA still in demand?

Demand varies by country and sector, so we will not quote figures. What is stable is the need behind it: organisations still need independent assurance over IT controls, and ISACA continues to update the job practice to reflect current tasks.

## Can I take the exam online?

Yes. ISACA offers the exam at PSI testing centres and by remote proctoring.

## Is there a penalty for guessing?

No. ISACA's candidate guide says there are no penalties for incorrect answers. Answer every question.
